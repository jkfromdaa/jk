## 1. Definition

**Definition (verbatim, Q3 2026 earnings release, 2026-11-10) [P, T1, S1]:**
> "Revenue backlog was approximately $132.5 billion as of September 30, 2026."
> "Revenue backlog includes remaining performance obligations, plus other amounts we estimate will be recognized as revenue in future periods under committed customer contracts, in each case, subject to the satisfaction of delivery and availability of service requirements."

The same footnote wording appears in the Q3 2025, Q4 2025, Q1 2026 and Q2 2026 releases [P, S5–S8].

**"Revenue backlog" is not a GAAP or 10-K/10-Q term.** A text search of the FY2025 10-K and the Q2 2026 10-Q finds no use of "backlog" as a metric; the filings disclose only RPO [P, S2, S3]. Backlog appears in earnings releases, calls and investor presentations.

**RPO definition (verbatim, Q2 2026 10-Q, Note 2) [P, T1, S2]:**
> "RPO represents the aggregate amount of the transaction price, net of estimated variable consideration, allocated to performance obligations not delivered, or partially undelivered, as of the end of the reporting period. Variable consideration primarily consists of potential reductions to the transaction price in the future, such as estimates of future potential credits to customers under availability of service agreements, amounts that may not be recognized as revenue due to delivery delays, and estimates of committed cloud computing capacity that the Company has the right to resell. ... RPO includes both billed and unbilled consideration from the Company's committed contracts."

So RPO is already reduced by management's estimate of SLA credits, delivery-delay losses and capacity it may resell. The size of that estimate is not disclosed.

**Split of the Q3 2026 backlog:** RPO $132.3B + other amounts $0.2B = $132.5B. [S, T1-via-T3: attributed by TradingKey/Investing.com/MarketScreener to the Q3'26 earnings presentation, which I could not open; S30.] For comparison, June 30, 2026: RPO $103.7B + $0.5B other = $104.2B [P, Sept 2026 investor presentation filed on Form 8-K, S9; RPO also in XBRL, S10].

**Caveats stated by the company:**
- "Revenue backlog is expected to fluctuate from period to period, given the nature of our committed contract business and the size of committed contracts." [P, T1, S9]
- Forward-looking-statement legend lists "other estimated amounts included in our revenue backlog figure" as a forward-looking estimate [P, S1].
- Risk factor: "in future periods, we may experience fluctuations in remaining performance obligations, given the nature of our committed contract business, the size of those contracts, and period-to-period variation in new business signed and revenue recognized from existing contracts." [P, S2]
- Every commitment in the 8-Ks is worded "Subject to any termination described below and satisfaction of delivery and availability of service requirements" and "up to approximately" [P, S11–S14].
- No sentence saying "backlog is not a guarantee of revenue" was found in the documents read.

Committed (take-or-pay) contracts were 98% of revenue in Q2 2026 and in FY2025 (96% in 2024, 88% in 2023) [P, S2, S3].

### Management statements on speed of conversion

- Q3 2026 call: "Of this backlog, more than 50% is attached to a contract where we expect customer delivery to commence by the end of this year." [P, S24]
- Q2 2026 call: "Of the existing backlog, more than 50% is attached to a contract where customer delivery has commenced. We expect this figure to reach more than 2/3 of our Q2 backlog by the end of this year." [P, S23] INFERRED: 2/3 of $104.2B ≈ $69B ≈ 52% of $132.5B, consistent with the Q3 statement; the early-Q3 additions are therefore mostly for deliveries starting 2027 or later.
- Q1 2026 call: backlog is "all tied to contracts that are either online today or expected to begin to come online through 2026 and 2027"; new Q1 commitments "mostly contribute towards our 2027 targets" [P, S21].
- Q4 2025 call: "every contract for new capacity expected to begin generating revenue by year-end 2026"; contracted power of 3.1 GW "virtually all of which we expect to come online by 2027" [P, S20].
- Revenue start: "When we deploy capacity and we have deployed, tested our GPUs and when we handed them over to the end customer is when we start recognizing revenue ... on a straight-line basis through the life of the customer contract." Fit-out takes "about 1 to 2 months"; "By month 3 in these deployments, we are typically generating revenue" [P, S21].
- Illustrative build timeline: data-center partner development and construction 12–18 months, then CoreWeave GPU installation and testing 1–2 months; "actual delivery dates and timelines are subject to a number of different factors" [P, S9].
- Q3 2026: "We also do not see material risks to our 2027 deliveries based on ongoing construction at fully permitted sites." [P, S24]

## 7. Conditions that void or reduce a contract — verbatim

### 7.1 Take-or-pay and prepayment mechanics
- "We currently sell access to our platform either through committed contracts, which are take-or-pay, or on-demand, which are pay-as-you-go. Committed contracts generally have a predetermined term and start either on a fixed date or when we deliver the capacity specified in the contract." [P, S2]
- "Take-or-pay. Contract arrangements where the customer commits to paying for a minimum specified amount of services or usage, regardless of whether they fully utilize those services." [P, S15]
- "As of December 31, 2025, 2024, and 2023, the weighted-average prepayment across all our active contracts was 15% to 25% of the TCV." [P, S2]
- Q3 2026 call: "This year, we have collected prepayments on 70% of our deals with customers funding up to 100% of the contract value upfront." [P, S24]
- "There is no guarantee that in the future customers will continue to be willing to enter into, and that the industry will continue to support, a take-or-pay model" [P, S2]

### 7.2 Termination rights — no termination for convenience appears in any filed MSA; termination is for cause or insolvency
- **Microsoft MSA §10(b)** [P, S43]: "A Party may terminate this Agreement (including each Order Form) for cause (i) upon forty-five (45) days written notice to the other Party of a material breach if such material breach remains uncured at the expiration of such period, or (ii) if the other Party becomes the subject of a petition in bankruptcy or any other proceeding relating to insolvency, receivership, liquidation or assignment for the benefit of creditors."
- **Microsoft MSA §10(c)**: "If this Agreement or any Order Form is terminated by Customer in accordance with Section 10(b) ..., CoreWeave will refund Customer any prepaid fees covering the remainder of the term of all applicable Order Forms after the effective date of termination." If CoreWeave terminates: "in no event will Customer pay any fees (whether by setoff or otherwise) for any Services that Customer does not receive from CoreWeave". If CoreWeave terminates for non-payment, uncurable violation of law or gross negligence and stops service: "Customer shall pay the lesser of (I) fees due for the remainder of the term minus proceeds from CoreWeave's transfer or sale of the Contract Servers, or (II) from the commencement date of this Agreement through the termination date, the difference between fees paid and fees charged at CoreWeave's on-demand rates." CoreWeave must "use all reasonable efforts to transfer or sell the remaining Contract Services ... to minimize the fees payable by Customer".
- **OpenAI MSA (2025-05-08) §10(b)** [P, S44]: "A Party may terminate this Agreement (including each Order Form) for cause (i) upon [*] days' written notice to the other Party of a material breach of this Agreement [*] if such material breach remains uncured at the expiration of such period (provided the cure period for nonpayment of undisputed fees for the Services shall be [*], or (ii) if the other Party becomes the subject of a petition in bankruptcy or any other proceeding relating to insolvency, receivership, liquidation or assignment for the benefit of creditors. ## 9. Outlook

**Guidance (Q3 2026 release and call) [P, S1, S24]:** 2026 revenue $13.0–13.3B (prior $12.4–13.2B; original $12–13B); 2026 capex $37–39B (prior $35–39B; original $30–35B); active power >1.85 GW at year-end; exit-2026 annualized run-rate revenue $20–21B; Q4 interest expense $1.05–1.15B. INFERRED: implied Q4 2026 revenue ≈ $4.7–5.0B.

**2027 and beyond:**
- Exit-2027 run-rate revenue "more than $30 billion", "more than 75% of which is already contracted, excluding any benefit from not-yet-exercised customer renewals" (Q1 2026) [P, S21]; Q3 2026: can "meaningfully exceed our ARR target for 2027" [P, S24].
- "Should we choose to focus only on our committed backlog, we would expect CapEx for 2027 to remain in line with our 2026 CapEx investment while revenue and EBITDA grow significantly." [P, S24]
- "We are largely sold out through 2027"; "10x as much qualified demand as available capacity through the end of 27"; 2028 capacity not yet formally marketed [P, S24].
- Target "at least 8 gigawatts by 2030" [P, S23].

**Renewals and re-contracting (outside backlog):**
- "More than 130 thousand GPUs come off contract in 2027 as do another approximately 170 thousand GPUs in 2028. ... At current pricing levels, 2027 renewals would generate approaching 4 billion of incremental annualized revenue with no additional CapEx required. This grows to nearly 10 billion ... when including 2028 renewals." CFO: "Those are outside our backlog" [P, S24].
- "$9 billion in prepayments and deposits" expected in the next three months, including "more than 3 billion that relate to recontracting existing cloud infrastructure" [P, S24].
- Q2 2026: capacity up for renewal "is a very limited part of our fleet"; "we have built a business whose economics do not rely on recontracting after initial customer term"; an A100 contract was signed that "extends into 2029" [P, S23].

**Pricing:** +25% across SKUs in July 2026, then +10% later in Q3; short-term capacity "more than 40 million per megawatt annually" (3–6 month contracts per the Sept 2026 slide) versus "20 million to 25 million per megawatt" on new 4–5-year contracts; Ampere/Hopper pricing up "30% to 50% quarter over quarter" [P, S24, S9]. The Sept 2026 slide's "~70% price increase across SKUs in July 2026" conflicts with the 25% + 10% stated on calls [P, S9 vs S23/S24] — unresolved.

**Mix shift:** more capacity is being allocated to short-term contracts and managed inference; DDTL 5.5 finances shorter-duration contracts; "enterprise tends to want ... 2 years or 3 years" [P, S23]. INFERRED: shorter contracts add less backlog per dollar of annual revenue, so backlog growth can slow while revenue grows.

**Risks flagged by management in Q3 2026:** "recent volatility in the capital markets"; "the seismic regulatory shift that is occurred" after the U.S. elections; Texas — "the overwhelming majority of our power is either online or fully permitted and under construction" [P, S24].

## 10. What the evidence supports on "grow, replace or reduce" (INFERRED)

1. Backlog is a stock that falls by revenue recognized (about $3.7B in Q3 2026, about $4.7–5.0B implied for Q4, $20–21B annualized at year-end) and rises only with new signed commitments. It has risen every quarter since IPO, but by amounts ranging from $4.2B to $32.6B.
2. About 36% of the $132.5B (≈ $48B) is scheduled for the 24 months to September 2028, 38% for months 25–48, 26% later; contracts end between 2029 and December 2032.
3. Older contracts ending in 2027–2028 are not in backlog until re-signed. Management quantifies that opportunity ($4B, rising to $10B annualized) at current prices; it is not contracted.
4. Reductions to date have been timing (one disclosed delay, customer consent to move dates), not cancellations. The contractual routes to a real reduction are: customer termination for uncured material breach or repeated availability failures; prolonged force majeure (30 days for OpenAI); change of control to a competitor (Microsoft, Meta); customer insolvency or non-payment; and SLA credits. Delivery-date remedies are in unfiled order forms.
